Coastal Geotechnics · Physics-Informed ML · Remote Sensing

Nripojyoti Biswas, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor, Civil & Environmental Engineering · University of Massachusetts Lowell

I study how coastal geo-infrastructure fails under storms, erosion, and changing hydrology, and how to see it coming. My group couples granular mechanics with physics-informed machine learning, satellite and robotic sensing, and laboratory experiments to build digital twins for bluffs, foundations, bridges, and embankments.

What I work on

Research

01

Physics-informed digital twins

Coupling granular mechanics with physics-informed neural networks to predict stability of coastal bluffs, foundations, bridge piers, and road and rail embankments under extreme loading, scour, and erosion, validated with new laboratory experiments.

02

Remote & robotic sensing

Integrating NISAR/InSAR, satellite soil-moisture products, UAV, LiDAR, and autonomous robotic platforms for early detection of infrastructure failure and post-storm reconnaissance.

03

Compound coastal hazards & adaptation

Hydrologic change during erosion and scour under storm surge, extreme rainfall, groundwater rise, and freeze–thaw; nature-based solutions and beneficial use of dredged material.

04

Community-based coastal resilience

Co-developing research and decision tools with local communities, regional planning agencies, tribal governments, and state agencies.

Other ongoing work: dynamic behavior of mine tailings, and remote-sensing-based prediction of earthquake-induced excess pore-water pressure. Earlier work centered on sustainable ground improvement of expansive and sulfate-rich soils, geosynthetics for pavements, and low-carbon stabilizers.
